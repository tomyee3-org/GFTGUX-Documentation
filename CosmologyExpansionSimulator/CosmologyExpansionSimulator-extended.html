The question for this laboratory: If we know a universe's present-day expansion rate, what else must we know to predict its age and future? Run the four commands in order. In each beat, do the tasks at the terminal before using the numbered checks as a worked explanation.

Before the first run

Install NumPy and Matplotlib, open a terminal in the program folder and run python main.py --version. Check the version and build against the line above. Each command below displays a Matplotlib window; --outdir ./figures also saves a timestamped PNG. In an interactive window, the lower-right cursor readout gives the plotted coordinates under your pointer. Read axis labels and units before interpreting a slope. Run python main.py --help for all options. --csvdir ./data saves numerical data and metadata; --no_plot --csvdir ./data runs without a figure.

Three clocks: H0 has units km/s/Mpc, 1/H0 is a time scale, and age today is the computed time from a finite-age Big Bang to a=1. A past-eternal model has no finite Big-Bang age. Time on the plot is in Gyr; a=1 denotes today in each model.

Beat 0 · One measured rate, a full expansion history

Three tasks. (1) From the summary, record the age, the derived curvature term, and the radiation-to-matter equality redshift. (2) On the density-budget panel, find which component dominates at the beginning, near a=1, and far into the plotted future. (3) Find where q crosses zero; does the universe begin accelerating before or after matter–dark-energy equality?

python main.py --mode evolve --H0 67.4 --omega_m 0.315 --outdir ./figures

Check your run. The summary prints an age of 13.79 Gyr, Omega_k0=0, matter–radiation equality z=3408, acceleration transition z=0.6317, and matter–dark-energy equality z=0.2955. Larger redshift is earlier: acceleration begins before the two densities become equal. Today q0=-0.5273.

ODE Then the equation. The program integrates (H/H0)² = Ωm0/a³ + Ωr0/a⁴ + Ωk0/a² + ΩDE0 g(a), with H=(1/a) da/dt. At a=1, these terms sum to one. Omitting --omega_de solves that closure equation for ΩDE0 = 1 − Ωm0 − Ωr0 and forces Ωk0=0. The default radiation term is 9.24e-5, so supplying --omega_de 0.685 explicitly would create a small Ωk0=-9.24e-5 instead of the intended exactly flat model.

DERIVED For w0=-1, wa=0, g(a)=1. Matter and radiation dilute as a⁻³ and a⁻⁴. The acceleration test uses the acceleration equation; equal matter and dark-energy densities alone do not locate q=0.

Beat 1 · Can a high expansion rate coexist with old stars?

Three tasks. (1) Identify the horizontal 13 Gyr reference line and read where the computed age crosses it. (2) Read the maximum and minimum ages and their scanned matter densities from the terminal. (3) Inspect any recollapse markers and ask how far into the future the scan actually checks.

python main.py --mode age --scan_param omega_m --scan_lo 0.05 --scan_hi 1.5 --scan_n 50 --outdir ./figures

Check your run. At fixed H0=70, the 50 flat-model scan points range from 20.75 Gyr at Ωm0=0.05 to 8.105 Gyr at Ωm0=1.5. The line crossing is around Ωm0≈0.35 (read the figure for the approximate intersection; the plotted points are discrete). Seventeen points reach a=1 and then recollapse before a=5; the marker does not certify the fate of every unmarked model.

DERIVED / NUMERICAL The age of a finite-age model is t0 = ∫₀¹ da/[a H0 E(a)]. The program starts at a_i=1e-8, adds an analytic early-time offset for a suitable dominant component, then integrates numerically. The dimensionless panel shows H0 t0; an exactly flat, matter-only Einstein–de Sitter model gives 2/3. This scan retains the default radiation term and compensates dark energy to keep flatness, so even a point at Ωm0=1 is not the exact matter-only EdS preset.

The scan's recollapse look-ahead stops at a=5; it can miss a turnaround beyond that limit. For detailed fate and warnings, rerun one chosen point in --mode evolve.

Beat 2 · Equal Hubble rates need not mean equal ages

Three tasks. (1) Compare the four ages in the terminal table. (2) Find the marked a=1 crossing for each plotted history. (3) Decide which model has an eventual Big Rip, which recollapses, and which keeps expanding with a cosmological constant.

python main.py --mode compare --presets EdS,lambdaCDM,closed,phantom --outdir ./figures

Check your run. The preset ages are EdS 9.312 Gyr, flat lambdaCDM 13.79 Gyr, closed 8.538 Gyr, and phantom 14.15 Gyr. Their parameter sets are different; the figure compares their scale-factor histories, q and dark energy fractions. The table labels the closed model recollapses and the phantom model Big Rip ahead. The latter is a future classification, not a claim that the plotted curve itself reaches infinite a.

CLOSURE / DERIVED The named presets are chosen teaching parameter sets. Once they are chosen, the shared Friedmann equation determines the histories numerically. The acceleration equation gives q = ½Ωm(a) + Ωr(a) + ½[1+3w(a)]ΩDE(a); spatial curvature has no direct stress-energy term in that equation. A model's q0 and its age therefore need not rank together.

Beat 3 · A turnaround changes the sign of H

Three tasks. (1) Find the maximum scale factor and time in the summary. (2) Follow the expansion and mirrored contraction branches and find the sign change in the Hubble panel. (3) Compare the quoted lifetime to twice the turnaround time; where does the plotted contracting branch stop?

python main.py --mode evolve --omega_m 1.5 --omega_de 0.0 --continue_collapse --outdir ./figures

Check your run. With Ωm0=1.5 and ΩDE0=0, the derived curvature is Ωk0=-0.50009. The age at a=1 is 8.538 Gyr; turnaround occurs near a=2.9995 at 93.06 Gyr. The estimated Big-Bang-to-Big-Crunch lifetime is 186.1 Gyr. The generated contracting trajectory stops at a_i, not at the a=0 singularity.

ODE / SYMMETRY At a genuine turnaround H²=H0² E(a)²=0. The same Friedmann evolution can be reflected in time to draw a contracting branch, with H<0; this time-reversal factor of two is exact for this model, while the computed turnaround time carries numerical error. Because q divides by H², it is undefined at the turnaround; do not read the clipped curve as a finite physical deceleration there.

Try it yourself · graded experiments

  1. EXP-1, closure: Rerun Beat 0 with an explicit --omega_de 0.685. Predict and then read the sign of Ωk0. Explain why the two commands are different models.
  2. EXP-2, independent age check: Run python main.py --mode evolve --omega_m 1 --omega_r 0 --omega_de 0. Compare the printed age to 2/(3H0) in Gyr using the printed Hubble time. Give a numerical difference.
  3. EXP-3, curvature: Run python main.py --mode age --scan_param omega_m --scan_lo 0.2 --scan_hi 1.2 --scan_n 20 --no_force_flat. At fixed other densities, does increasing Ωm0 now alter curvature?
  4. EXP-4, future bound: Rerun Beat 3 with --a_max 2 added. Compare the stop reason, plotted range and reported fate. Does an a_max stop certify the absence of a later turnaround? Contrast this run with the full Beat 3 run.
  5. EXP-5, convergence: Rerun Beat 0 with --step_frac 0.0025. Compare the age printed to four significant figures. Does one unchanged printed number establish arbitrary accuracy?
  6. EXP-6, evolving dark energy: Run python main.py --mode evolve --w0 -1 --wa 0.2 --a_max 2. At a=1, what is w? What does the CPL formula predict at a=2? Do not extrapolate this fit to all future time.
  7. EXP-7, phantom: Run python main.py --mode evolve --w0 -1.2 --a_max 60. Find the estimated Big Rip time in the printout; does the figure include infinite scale factor?
  8. EXP-8, past-eternal: Run python main.py --mode evolve --omega_m 0 --omega_r 0 --omega_de 1 --a_i 0.01. What does the printout put in the age field? Repeat at smaller a_i and distinguish elapsed time from a physical age.

Parameters, outputs and method

The evolve mode prints input and derived densities, finite-age status, epoch crossings, fate estimates where established, and an explicit stop reason. Its four-panel figure shows a(t), H(t), density fractions versus a, and q(t). A genuine turnaround has undefined fractional densities and q at that point. The compare mode overlays named parameter sets and prints their age and fate table. The age mode scans one parameter; --scan_param accepts omega_m, omega_de, w0 or H0. Its recollapse flag searches only through a=5. The terminal summaries are rounded: use --csvdir for numerical study. Without --omega_de, evolve is flat by construction; --no_force_flat applies to age scans.

The expansion trajectory uses a fixed fraction of the local Hubble time for RK4 stepping, with special endpoint and root handling; this is not a general error-controlled integrator. A smaller --step_frac provides a practical convergence check. --a_i sets the positive start of integration, --a_max and --t_max are stopping bounds for evolve/compare, and --continue_collapse displays the reflected contracting branch. See python main.py --help for validation ranges and output options.

What these figures can establish

ODE The first and second Friedmann equations hold within an FLRW perfect-fluid model. FIT w(a)=w0+wa(1−a) is a phenomenological CPL prescription; the associated dark-energy density history g(a)=a^[-3(1+w0+wa)] exp[-3wa(1−a)] follows from that choice. Its far-future extrapolation and all extreme toy parameter choices need special care. NUMERICAL The plotted points and fate estimates have finite precision and integration bounds. The model describes a homogeneous expansion background; it does not simulate galaxies, perturbations, dark-energy microphysics or the quantum origin of a Big Bang. A model can be mathematically accepted without being a plausible description of our universe.
