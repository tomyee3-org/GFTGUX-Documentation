COSMOLOGY_EXPANSION_SIMULATOR — REPRESENTATIVE SAMPLE OUTPUTS
Program version: 1.1.0
Prepared: 2026-08-23 (revised after a third round of independent AI code review)

Purpose
-------
These four figures give a quick visual survey of the program's three modes.
The program evaluates the FLRW background equations for a homogeneous,
isotropic universe given matter, radiation, curvature, and dark-energy
density parameters, and solves the resulting expansion history numerically.
The governing equations are exact within that model; the numerical output
carries ordinary (small, but non-zero) integration, root-finding, and
quadrature error. The one phenomenological ingredient is the dark-energy
equation of state w(a) = w0 + wa(1-a), a placeholder for physics nobody yet
understands.

1. FLAT LCDM (PLANCK-2018-LIKE CONCORDANCE COSMOLOGY)
   Figure: cosmo_evolve_20260823_095947_121005.png

   Command:
   python main.py --mode evolve --H0 67.4 --omega_m 0.315 \
       --outdir ./figures
   (omitting --omega_de forces flatness exactly; passing --omega_de 0.685
   explicitly would leave a stray Omega_k0 = -9.24e-5 from the default
   radiation density)

   What it shows:
   - Scale-factor growth a(t) from deep radiation domination to today and
     beyond, with the present epoch marked.
   - The Hubble parameter H(t) falling by many orders of magnitude; for
     this flat-LCDM case specifically it flattens toward its de Sitter
     asymptote as dark energy comes to dominate (that flattening is
     specific to a cosmological constant / de Sitter domination, not a
     general feature of every dark-energy case this program can run).
   - The radiation -> matter -> dark-energy sequence of Omega_i(a), with
     the sum equal to 1 throughout by algebraic construction (a bookkeeping
     identity, not an independent check on the integration).
   - The deceleration parameter q(t) crossing zero at the onset of
     accelerated expansion.

   Headline values:
   - Age of the universe: 13.79 Gyr.
   - Matter-radiation equality: z = 3408.
   - Deceleration/acceleration transition: z = 0.632.
   - q0 = -0.527.
   - Run stopped because: a_max reached.

2. A CLOSED, RECOLLAPSING UNIVERSE
   Figure: cosmo_evolve_20260823_095955_089616.png

   Command:
   python main.py --mode evolve --omega_m 1.5 --omega_de 0.0 \
       --continue_collapse --outdir ./figures

   What it shows:
   - A matter-dominated, positively curved (closed) universe that expands,
     turns around, and recollapses.
   - The contracting branch, obtained not by re-integration but by the
     exact time-reversal symmetry of the Friedmann equation -- mirrored
     back down to a_i, not literally to the a=0 singularity -- with H(t)
     correctly negative there (H = a_dot/a flips sign; the plot uses a
     symmetric-log axis so the sign change is visible rather than clipped).
   - The deceleration parameter diverging as the turnaround is approached
     (H -> 0 while the densities stay finite).

   Headline values:
   - Turnaround: a_turn = 2.9995 at t = 93.06 Gyr, located by direct
     bisection on the algebraic condition E(a)^2=0, with the turnaround
     TIME found by a singularity-removing quadrature. Verified against an
     independently coded quadrature benchmark. --a_max and --t_max, when
     given, are enforced as hard stopping bounds even when either is
     requested exactly at, or arbitrarily close to, the true turnaround
     (the numerically hardest case, and the specific defect a third round
     of independent review found and this revision fixes; see the
     accompanying response document).
   - Total lifetime, Big Bang to Big Crunch: 186.1 Gyr.
   - Age at our own present size (a=1): 8.538 Gyr.
   - Run stopped because: a genuine turnaround (not a_max or t_max).

3. FOUR TEXTBOOK COSMOLOGIES, COMPARED
   Figure: cosmo_compare_20260823_100005_428361.png

   Command:
   python main.py --mode compare --presets EdS,lambdaCDM,closed,phantom \
       --outdir ./figures

   What it shows:
   - Overlaid scale-factor histories, each marked where it crosses a=1.
   - Overlaid deceleration histories (axis deliberately clipped so the
     closed model's divergence near turnaround does not swamp the others).
   - A direct bar-chart comparison of ages, with recollapsing models
     flagged rather than silently omitted.
   - Overlaid dark-energy fractions, showing how late a phantom and a
     constant-Lambda model actually diverge from each other.
   - Also available (not pictured): --presets lambdaCDM,evolving_de,
     phantom,desi_like isolates the effect of the dark-energy equation of
     state alone, holding H0 and Omega_m0 fixed across all four (EXP-9).
     desi_like (w0=-0.7, wa=-1.0) illustrates -- but is not a fit to --
     the w0>-1, wa<0 quadrant favored by DESI DR2 (2025); note that it has
     the OPPOSITE sign of wa from evolving_de (w0=-0.8, wa=+0.3).

4. THE HISTORICAL "AGE CRISIS": AGE VS. OMEGA_M
   Figure: cosmo_age_scan_20260823_100029_976039.png

   Command:
   python main.py --mode age --scan_param omega_m --scan_lo 0.05 \
       --scan_hi 1.5 --scan_n 50 --outdir ./figures

   What it shows:
   - Age of the universe as a function of Omega_m (flat by construction),
     crossing the reference 13 Gyr globular-cluster age near Omega_m
     of about 0.3-0.4.
   - The dimensionless H0 t0 product, with the classic Einstein-de Sitter
     (2/3) and Milne (1) reference lines.
   - The pre-1998 realization that Omega_m = 1 makes the universe younger
     than its own oldest stars, made quantitative and reproducible. Points
     with Omega_m > 1 (forced flat, so Omega_DE0 = 1 - Omega_m0 < 0) still
     reach a=1 fine but are flagged as recollapsing within this scan's
     fixed a=5 look-ahead horizon (CSV column recollapses_by_a5) -- a
     model with a genuine a_turn beyond that horizon would be reported as
     not recollapsing here, not confirmed to expand forever.

Notes for evaluation
---------------------
- All runs also print detailed terminal summaries, including which of
  a_max, t_max, or a genuine turnaround actually stopped the run, and a
  "notes on this run" block whenever a model has been pushed outside
  typical bounds.
- Adding --csvdir PATH writes timestamped, self-documenting CSV tables
  (17 significant digits for t_Gyr and a, so that even closely spaced
  rows near a turnaround remain numerically distinguishable), including
  a comment block with both the input parameters and the actual derived
  results (stop_reason, the derived Omega_k0, age, and turnaround, when
  applicable) for that run, without suppressing the screen display.
- Adding --outdir PATH saves each timestamped figure and still displays it.
- --no_plot --csvdir PATH provides a data-only mode for parameter sweeps.
- The full help file explains which results are exact GR, which are exact
  algebraic identities, which are asymptotic or numerical, and which rest
  on the one phenomenological ingredient (the dark-energy equation of
  state) -- and supplies 20-plus exercises from introductory through
  advanced, including exercises that exercise the a_max/t_max/turnaround
  precedence logic directly, up to and including an exact closed-form
  cycloid benchmark for the closed matter-only case.
- This revision follows a THIRD independent round of AI code review
  (Microsoft Copilot, Google Gemini, and OpenAI Codex each independently
  re-audited the second round's repairs). It replaces the previous
  boundary-resolution method -- which used a single large numerical
  integration step to reach a requested a_max, t_max, or checkpoint near
  a turning point -- with one based entirely on quadrature once a genuine
  turning point is known to be nearby, fixing several related failures
  found near that boundary: a crash or a large silent time error when
  a_max sits at or very near a true turnaround; a similar failure mode for
  t_max, including an internal exception that could otherwise reach the
  user; a corrupted age-of-the-universe value for a model whose turnaround
  falls just after a=1; and a case where checking only the sign of
  E(a_max)^2 could miss an earlier turning point when E(a)^2 has more than
  one positive root. The automated test suite (tests/test_physics_cosmo.py)
  has been substantially expanded to cover this boundary regime directly,
  including comparisons against independently implemented numerical
  cross-checks. See the accompanying response document for full details.
