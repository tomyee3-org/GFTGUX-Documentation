Beat 3 · Cold collapse and rebound

Exact command:

python main.py --mode galaxy --seed 3 --outdir ./figures --csvdir ./data

What it shows

  • The final positions show two separated clumps; the radii refer to the whole system and include their separation.
  • At deepest collapse, 2T/|U| is about 1.43 while |W_vir|/|U| is about 0.258, giving the plotted Q of 5.55.
  • The default softening is 44.48 pc, larger than the sampled minimum half-mass radius of 18.54 pc.
  • A near-unity global virial ratio and the finite-window classification do not establish complete equilibrium.

Headline values

  • Half-mass radius: 162.6 pc (initial) -> 18.54 pc (deepest collapse, at 55.6 Myr) -> 169.3 pc (final)
  • Virial ratio 2T/|W|: 0.0000 -> 0.9525 (at deepest collapse: 5.5517)
  • Free-fall time t_ff: 46.84 Myr
  • Max sampled energy drift: 1.697%

Beat 4 · Growth of a small initial displacement

Exact command:

What it shows

  • The approximately straight segment on the logarithmic plot supplies the fitted growth rate.
  • The two random seeds specify the original realization and its displacement separately.
  • The curve can level off when the separation becomes comparable to the cluster size.

Headline values

  • Initial separation: 2.716e-08 pc (relative perturbation 1.0e-08)

  • Fit used: 145 of 201 snapshots (R^2 = 0.99450, spanning 16.43 e-folds, curvature t = -6.40)
  • Max sampled energy drift: 0.003%

Notes for evaluation

  • Run each exact command from the folder containing main.py. The headline values and displayed image come from the same selected run. For the seed-2 comparison in Beat 1, repeat that section's command with --seed 2.
  • The CSV includes both provenance and the console summary before its data header. The CSV, PNG and .provenance.txt sidecar share one run-start timestamp and identifying suffix, even when their directories differ.
  • The Guide's results were produced on Linux with Python 3.12.14, NumPy 2.3.5 and Matplotlib 3.10.8. The Help retains Windows 11 Beat 0 values (Python 3.14.6, NumPy 2.5.0) (217.23% maximum energy error and 17 of 60 instantaneously unbound), while the Linux Beat 0 figure here records 278.212% and 12 of 60. Both runs fail energy conservation, so neither endpoint is a trustworthy physical escape count. The Windows Beat 5 ensemble is separately labelled in the Help. Long chaotic integrations can have different late-time endpoints across numerical environments; compare the settings and environment records when interpreting them.
  • An instantaneous positive-energy count is not a count of verified permanent escapes. A small sampled energy error does not alone establish accurate individual chaotic trajectories. A value near one for the virial ratio does not establish complete equilibrium.
