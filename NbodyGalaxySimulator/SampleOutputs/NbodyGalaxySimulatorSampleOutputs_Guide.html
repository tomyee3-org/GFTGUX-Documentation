Purpose. These four figures give a compact visual survey of the program's three modes. The simulator evolves a system of point masses under Newtonian gravity with Plummer softening, using either a Barnes–Hut octree approximation or direct O(N2) summation, and a kick-drift-kick (leapfrog) integrator. The governing physics is exact Newtonian gravity for softened point masses; the tree approximation, finite timestep, and finite softening each carry ordinary, explicitly bounded numerical error, which every run reports directly (see each figure's own energy-conservation panel). Figures 1 and 2 are a deliberate before/after pair at the same underlying physics (star-cluster relaxation) and are best read together.

What it shows

  • The virial ratio 2T/|W| oscillates around 1 with no secular drift, and the instantaneously-unbound count and near-escape tail both stay at zero except for one brief, transient blip.

2. Star-cluster relaxation, tuned to reveal two-body evaporation

Figure: cluster_20260905_181313.png

python main.py --mode cluster --n_bodies 60 --softening_pc 0.0338 \
  --steps_per_crossing 150 --n_relax 40 --seed 0

Representative output generated for this guide and embedded directly, reduced to a 256-color indexed palette.

What it shows

  • The same physics as Figure 1, but with softening lowered roughly sevenfold, N reduced, and the run lengthened to 40 relaxation times — the Help file's own prescription for making two-body relaxation actually visible within a practical run.
  • The Lagrangian radii now visibly diffuse outward, most clearly the 90% shell, rather than staying flat.
  • The virial ratio oscillates far more energetically, and both the instantaneously-unbound count and the near-escape tail come alive well before the end of the run, with one body still unbound at the final snapshot.
  • Energy conservation is still good (0.33% max sampled drift) despite the smaller softening length and much longer integration.

Headline values

  • Half-mass radius: 1.424 → 1.301 pc, with the 90% shell diffusing well outward (see figure).
  • Virial ratio 2T/|W|: 1.0000 → 1.0483 (oscillating throughout, not monotonic).
  • Instantaneously unbound: 0 → 1 of 60 bodies (1.67%); near-escape tail reaches 3.33% by the end.
  • Softening: 0.0338 pc (explicit); max sampled energy drift: 0.33%.

3. Cold-collapse galaxy formation: violent relaxation into a remnant

Figure: galaxy_20260905_181416.png

python main.py --mode galaxy --seed 3

Representative output generated for this guide and embedded directly, reduced to a 256-color indexed palette.

What it shows

  • A 300-body cold (initial virial ratio 0), uniform sphere collapsing under its own gravity, reaching a sharp density spike at deepest collapse, then "violently relaxing" (Lynden-Bell 1967) into a settled remnant.
  • The Lagrangian radii collapse together to a common minimum, then separate and level off as the system virializes — the classic collapse-overshoot-settle signature.
  • The virial ratio spikes sharply above 1 at deepest collapse (a strongly non-equilibrium instant) and then damps down to oscillate around 1.
  • The program's own numerically defined settled-remnant classifier judged this run's final quarter both amplitude-quiet and centered near Q=1, consistent with (not proof of) a genuine quasi-equilibrium remnant.

Headline values

  • Half-mass radius: 162.6 pc (initial) → 18.5 pc (deepest collapse, t = 55.6 Myr) → 169.3 pc (final).
  • Virial ratio 2T/|W|: 0.000 → 5.552 at deepest collapse → 0.953 final.
  • Late-time window: mean half-mass radius 8.9× the run's global minimum; mean Q within 0.087 of exact virial balance.
  • Softening: 44.48 pc (default scaling); max sampled energy drift: 1.70%.

4. Sensitivity to initial conditions: a textbook exponential divergence

Figure: chaos_20260905_181438.png

python main.py --mode chaos --seed 1

Representative output generated for this guide and embedded directly, reduced to a 256-color indexed palette.

What it shows

  • Two 40-body realizations of the same Plummer cluster, integrated identically from initial positions offset by one part in 108, using direct summation for maximum accuracy.
  • Their RMS separation grows over 15 clean e-folds on a log scale, essentially a straight line, before saturating once the two realizations are as far apart as the cluster itself.
  • The program's automatic fit window (green) isolates the exponential-growth portion for a finite-time Lyapunov-exponent estimate, reporting a strong R² = 0.998 fit over 153 of 201 snapshots.
  • Both realizations conserve energy to within a few parts in 107, confirming the divergence is a genuine sensitivity effect, not integration error.

Headline values

  • RMS separation: 2.7×10-8 pc → 1.96 pc (peak 2.41 pc).
  • Estimated Lyapunov exponent: 0.281 Myr-1; Lyapunov time: 3.56 Myr (5.4 crossing times).
  • Fit quality: R² = 0.998 over 153 of 201 snapshots, spanning 16.4 e-folds.
  • Method: direct summation (this mode's default, for maximum accuracy); max sampled energy drift: 0.003%.

Notes for evaluation

  • Every run prints a detailed terminal summary in physical units (pc, Myr, M⊙), including plain-language caveats on what each diagnostic does and does not establish — e.g. "instantaneously unbound" is a snapshot count, not a cumulative escape total, and a virial ratio near 1 is called necessary but not sufficient for genuine dynamical equilibrium.
  • --outdir PATH saves a timestamped PNG together with a plain-text provenance sidecar recording every parameter actually used, the program version/build, and the runtime environment.
  • --csvdir PATH writes the full per-snapshot diagnostic time series to a timestamped CSV file for independent analysis, without suppressing the screen display.
  • --no_plot together with --csvdir supports a data-only workflow for parameter sweeps or batch runs.
  • --method {tree,direct} lets a user directly compare the Barnes–Hut approximation against exact direct summation at the same N; cluster and galaxy mode default to the tree, chaos mode defaults to direct summation for maximum accuracy.
  • Figures 1 and 2 above are a deliberate paired illustration of a point the Help file makes explicitly: this program's own default softening, chosen for force accuracy, actively suppresses the two-body relaxation it can also be used to demonstrate, and showing that mechanism cleanly requires the tuned, non-default parameters used in Figure 2.
