Beat 3 · A finite-time chaos estimate from two seeded trajectories

Exact command:

python main.py --mode chaos --seed 1 --perturbation_seed 1 --outdir ./figures

What it shows

  • The log-separation trace grows over an automatically selected fit window.
  • The cluster and its displacement use separately specified seeds.
  • The fitted finite-time slope is a heuristic estimate and the separation eventually saturates.

Headline values

  • Initial separation: 2.716e-08 pc (relative perturbation 1.0e-08)
  • Lyapunov exponent: 0.2994 1/Myr (finite-time growth-rate ESTIMATE, not a certified value)
  • Lyapunov time: 3.34 Myr (5.07 x crossing times; i.e. the e-folding time of that same estimate)
  • Fit used: 145 of 201 snapshots (R^2 = 0.99450, spanning 16.43 e-folds, curvature t = -6.40)
  • Max sampled energy drift: 0.003%

Notes for evaluation

  • Execute each exact command from the directory containing main.py. The terminal results in the headline bullets are from the same run as the figure.
  • The cluster's instantaneously-unbound count is not cumulative; a different seed or softening can change it. The default cluster is quiet.
  • The galaxy verdict is a finite-window numerical check; a virial ratio near one by itself cannot certify equilibrium. Its W is the softened virial force term W_vir, not potential energy U. The chaos slope is a finite-time heuristic, not an asymptotic exponent.
  • Beat 3 sets --perturbation_seed as well as --seed: the perturbation seed otherwise defaults to unseeded and the figure is not numerically reproducible. --outdir also writes a PNG provenance sidecar. Check that sidecar when comparing environments: random-sampling details or plotted pixels can vary across NumPy and Matplotlib versions.
