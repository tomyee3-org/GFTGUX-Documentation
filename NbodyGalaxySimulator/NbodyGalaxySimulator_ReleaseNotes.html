Release Status

Version 1.0.1 concludes a kickoff development pass followed by ten rounds of independent review involving Codex, Copilot, Gemini and Grok. The reviewers verified the Barnes–Hut octree force approximation and direct pairwise summation against each other and against closed-form and high-precision oracles; the Plummer-softened gravity, leapfrog integration, and energy/virial-ratio bookkeeping (including the distinction between the unsoftened potential energy and the correct softened scalar-virial quantity); input validation and floating-point overflow, underflow, and subnormal behavior across an extensive range of extreme and boundary inputs; the star-cluster relaxation, cold-collapse galaxy-formation, and chaos/Lyapunov-sensitivity modes; plotting, CSV output, command-line behavior, Help-file accuracy, and Version/Build synchronization; and both the canonical and flattened upload layouts under a real Python 3.10 interpreter. The final review round's most recent findings were weighed directly for reachability within this program's own documented physical domain (see Known Limitations below); none of the remaining findings are reachable by ordinary or deliberately aggressive-but-physical student use, and the program is sufficiently robust, accurate, and clear for student use within the tutorial scope and limitations described below.

Major Improvements in This Release

  • Verified Newtonian point-mass gravity with Plummer softening, evaluated either by direct O(N2) pairwise summation or by a Barnes–Hut (1986) octree approximation, and confirmed the two methods agree with each other and with closed-form/high-precision references across ordinary, boundary, and many extreme-magnitude configurations.
  • Hardened the floating-point arithmetic underlying every force and energy calculation against intermediate overflow and underflow for physically representable inputs: scale-safe, order-independent summation for kinetic and potential energy and for direct-summation forces; an exact-rational (Fraction-based) fallback for reduction slices that would otherwise lose a representable result to rounding; and a center-of-mass-relative kinetic energy formulation that survives an overflowing raw velocity difference.
  • Distinguished the unsoftened potential energy from the physically correct softened scalar-virial quantity (Wvir) throughout the diagnostics and documentation, since the two disagree by a non-negligible amount once softening is active, and every caller now uses the quantity appropriate to what it is actually reporting.
  • Added a numerically defined, regression-tested classifier for whether a cold-collapse galaxy run has genuinely settled into a quasi-equilibrium remnant by the end of the simulation (rather than assuming it, or reporting a still-collapsing run as settled), calibrated against both a synthetic monotonic-expansion counterexample and a real still-collapsing configuration.
  • Consolidated and rate-limited the Barnes-Hut tree-depth "bucket" warning (emitted when several bodies are too close together for the octree to separate) so a dense cluster or multi-step integration reports the condition clearly, exactly once per relevant scope, with its own severity (bodies, buckets, and largest single clump affected), rather than flooding the console or losing detail across a run.
  • Verified momentum, energy-scale, and Galilean-boost invariance properties (permutation-, rotation-, and translation-invariance of the force calculation; boost-invariance of the official center-of-mass-relative virial ratio) with dedicated metamorphic regression tests.
  • Expanded the Help file into a progressive sequence of exercises covering cluster relaxation and evaporation, cold collapse and violent relaxation, chaos and Lyapunov-time estimation, softening and resolution sensitivity, and convergence studies with an explicit, unambiguous statistical comparison rule.
  • Added a reproducible BUILD_ID across the four executable source modules, with newline-independent hashing, damaged-source fail-fast behavior, and synchronization across source, Help file, runtime output (--version), and result metadata.
  • Validated behavior under both the canonical tests/ project layout and a flattened single-directory upload layout, and confirmed compatibility with Python 3.10 as the minimum supported interpreter.

Test Suite Growth

A 118-test regression suite was created at kickoff and expanded to 291 automated tests over ten review rounds. The suite defines the four core module filenames and locates the nearest directory containing all four, so the same test file works from the canonical tests/ directory or when flattened beside the program modules.

The final suite verifies physical constants and closed forms; input validation across ordinary, boundary, invalid, and extreme finite inputs, including binary64 overflow, subnormal, and underflow boundaries; direct and tree-based force calculation, including their agreement with each other and with independent oracles; energy, momentum, and virial-ratio bookkeeping, including the potential-energy-versus-Wvir distinction; Lagrangian radii, escaper tracking, and relaxation/dynamical timescales; Plummer-sphere and uniform-sphere initial conditions; leapfrog integration and its diagnostic time series; the late-time settled-remnant classifier against both positive and negative real-integration controls; chaos-mode Lyapunov-time estimation, including real multi-seed integration regression; permutation, rotation, translation, and Galilean-boost invariance properties; run-mode summary contracts, driver-level validation, CSV output, command-line behavior, and plotting; Help-file structure and scientific content; Python 3.10 compatibility; and independent Build-ID reproduction. All 291 tests pass under CPython 3.10.20 in both canonical and flattened upload layouts.

Known Limitations

  • Intended for education and conceptual exploration of gravitational N-body dynamics rather than research-grade stellar dynamics or production astrophysical simulation.
  • This is a from-scratch, transparent, pure-Python teaching implementation: no individual (per-body) or block timestepping, no higher-order symplectic integrator, no quadrupole or higher multipole correction in the tree (monopole only), no parallelism or GPU acceleration, no gas or radiative physics, no external tidal field or cosmological expansion, and every simulation particle carries the same mass.
  • The Barnes-Hut tree's usual O(N log N) cost reduction is typical for a reasonably well-distributed tree and a nonzero opening angle, not a worst-case guarantee; a degenerate, highly clumped distribution of near-coincident bodies, or an opening angle of zero, can approach the direct method's O(N2) cost instead. The tree's wall-clock speed advantage over direct summation is therefore not guaranteed at every body count this program targets; see the Help file's Algorithm section for measured numbers.
  • Two-body relaxation is a slow physical process that this program's own default softening length actively suppresses (softening chosen for force accuracy also damps the close encounters that physically drive relaxation), and a fixed global timestep only marginally resolves the sharp density spike at the deepest point of a cold collapse; both are called out as explicit exercises rather than hidden.
  • A small number of force- and energy-summation code paths (direct-summation acceleration, kinetic and potential energy, and the tree's leaf/monopole accumulators) can still return a silently wrong finite value, rather than the true representable result or a clean error, for combinations of individual body masses, positions, or velocities that differ from each other by roughly 250 to 300 or more orders of magnitude in SI units, or for a mass ratio between two bodies in the same simulation beyond roughly 1050–10100. These thresholds sit far outside any physically meaningful input this program's solar-mass/parsec-scale unit system and validated parameter ranges can produce — reaching them requires an individual input (a body mass, a displacement, a mass ratio) that is already physically nonsensical on its own terms, independent of any floating-point consideration. No ordinary or deliberately aggressive-but-physical student exercise reaches this boundary; it is recorded here, with its approximate reproduction recipe, for a future maintainer who does need to work outside this program's intended physical domain.
  • Under the same kind of extreme, physically nonsensical input mixture described above, the exact-rational (Fraction-based) fallback that protects ordinary reductions from losing a representable result can in principle be triggered repeatedly within one large array, which has not been separately load-tested for worst-case runtime cost; this is a performance question under the same unreachable-in-practice input regime, not a correctness one.
  • The Help file loads MathJax from a public CDN. Offline, its prose and unrendered TeX source remain available, but equations are not typeset.

Known Minor Maintenance Items

  • The late-time settled-remnant classifier's Q-range and Q-center tolerance bands, and the in-sample calibration basis for the chaos-mode Lyapunov-time significance gate, are documented as intentionally generous; a future round could tighten either against a wider, out-of-sample calibration campaign without changing the current pass/fail behavior of any existing regression fixture.
  • The chaos mode's three-seed real-integration regression test is the single largest contributor to the full suite's wall-clock time; a future round could replace its categorical side-of-threshold check with a continuous invariant-based one, which would likely run faster without weakening the check.
  • Process verification across this program's review history has been reported in narrative form; a future maintenance pass could generate a machine-readable verification artifact (a test manifest, JSON record, or checksum bundle) alongside the narrative report for independent, automated inspection.
  • A future documentation-wide overhaul may bundle MathJax locally if fully typeset offline Help becomes a distribution requirement.
