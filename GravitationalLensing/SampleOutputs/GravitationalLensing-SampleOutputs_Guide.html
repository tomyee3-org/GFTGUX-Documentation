Beat 0: Forward rays: one ring or two arriving directions

python main.py --mode rays --beta_x 0 --beta_y 0.35 --outdir ./figures --dpi 140

A forward thin-lens sketch contrasts the on-axis ring with two arriving rays from an off-axis source.

What it shows

  • On axis, rotational symmetry turns arriving directions into an Einstein ring.
  • Off axis, two ray paths reach the observer from opposite sides of the mass.
  • The side view is schematic; red rays arrive and blue rays miss.

Headline values

  • Lens mass: 10¹² M☉; assumed distances: 1 and 2 Gpc.
  • Einstein angle: 9.78321067 × 10⁻⁶ rad, or 2.018 arcsec.
  • Off-axis source height: 1.69684788 × 10⁻⁶ rad, or +0.350 arcsec.
  • Schematic geometry: Dₗ = Dₗₛ = 1 and Dₛ = 2 in drawing units.

Beat 1: A compact source through a point-mass lens

python main.py --mode point --beta_x 0.50 --beta_y 0 --outdir ./figures --dpi 140

A false-colour point-mass image shows the off-axis source as a bright and faint pair.

What it shows

  • The cyan circle marks the Einstein radius in the image plane.
  • The white cross is the unlensed source at 0.50 arcsec, inside that circle.
  • The outer image is brighter and has opposite parity to the fainter inner image.

Headline values

  • Einstein radius: 2.017932 arcsec; source: (+0.500000, 0) arcsec.
  • Ideal image positions: +2.283359 and −1.783359 arcsec along the source axis.
  • Signed ideal magnifications: +2.564097 and −1.564097.
  • Grid: 227 × 227 pixels over a 6.0000-arcsec field.
  • The other Beat 1 runs: on-axis gives a ring; logM = 12.5 with beta=(0.3,0.3) arcsec yields an Einstein radius of 3.588447 arcsec in a 9.4462-arcsec field.

An offset elliptical source produces an uneven, nearly complete ring under the point-mass mapping.

What it shows

  • At this source size and offset, the extended image is an uneven, nearly complete ring.
  • The point-mass lens equation is the same one used in Beats 1 and 2.
  • The cross marks the unlensed centre of the source.

Headline values

  • Einstein radius: 2.017932 arcsec; source: (+0.150000, 0) arcsec.
  • Source effective radius: 0.2500 arcsec; axis ratio: 0.6000.
  • Position angle: 30.00 degrees.
  • Grid: 181 × 181 pixels over a 6.0000-arcsec field.
  • The other Beat 3 runs: a centred source makes a thick ring; beta=(0.15,0.8) arcsec with R_e = 0.20 arcsec expands the field to 6.2103 arcsec.

Beat 4: The critical curve, its point caustic, and the rays

python main.py --mode critical --beta_x 0 --beta_y 0.4 --outdir ./figures --dpi 140

Three panels connect a sky-plane critical curve to its point caustic and a schematic ray slice.

What it shows

  • The critical circle lives in the image plane; its caustic is a point in the source plane.
  • A vertical source displacement changes the ray drawing on the right.
  • The side panel is a thin-lens cartoon, not a spacetime geodesic.

Headline values

  • Einstein radius: 2.017932 arcsec.
  • Source: (0, +0.400000) arcsec; the caustic is at (0, 0).
  • Compact-source width: 0.0800 arcsec.
  • Grid: 227 × 227 pixels over a 6.0000-arcsec field.
  • The other Beat 4 run (no source offset) puts the source on the caustic and produces a ring.

Beat 5: An SIS plus shear: diamond, folds, and cusps

python main.py --mode shear --beta_x 0.05 --beta_y 0.03 --outdir ./figures --dpi 140

The SIS plus shear model produces a diamond caustic and four images of the central source.

What it shows

  • The lens model is now a singular isothermal sphere with external shear.
  • Four discrete images correspond to a source inside the diamond caustic.
  • The diamond has different short- and long-axis cusp distances.

Headline values

  • SIS velocity dispersion: 300 km/s; Einstein radius: 1.297795 arcsec.
  • Shear γ = +0.250000; source: (+0.050000, +0.030000) arcsec; four images.
  • Critical-curve radial span: 1.038236 to 1.730393 arcsec.
  • Diamond short-axis cusp: 0.519118 arcsec; long-axis cusp: 0.865197 arcsec.
  • Other Beat 5 runs give 2 images at beta=(0.55,0.10) arcsec, 1 at (2.0,0), and a ring for gamma=0 at the origin.

Beat 6: Tangential arcs from an elliptical source

python main.py --mode arcs --beta_x 0.18 --beta_y 0 --outdir ./figures --dpi 140

An extended elliptical source creates arcs around the SIS plus shear critical curve.

What it shows

  • A finite source near a fold or cusp is distorted into arcs.
  • The cyan curve is the image-plane critical curve of the SIS plus shear lens.
  • False colour displays model intensity, not a wavelength-dependent deflection.

Headline values

  • Einstein radius: 1.297795 arcsec; shear γ = +0.250000.
  • Source: (+0.180000, 0) arcsec; effective radius: 0.2500 arcsec.
  • Source axis ratio: 0.6000; orientation: 30.00 degrees.
  • Grid: 181 × 181 pixels over a 6.0000-arcsec field.
  • Other Beat 6 runs: beta=(0.18,0.30) arcsec, R_e=0.14 samples a fold (217 pixels); beta_x=0.55, R_e=0.14 samples the short-axis cusp (6.3609-arcsec field); at the origin shear=0.25 shows four arcs and shear=0 a ring.

Beat 7: SIS convergence and the κ = 1/2 circle

python main.py --mode kappa --outdir ./figures --dpi 140

A false-colour convergence map marks the SIS radius where κ equals one half.

What it shows

  • The SIS convergence decreases as the inverse of angular distance from the lens.
  • The overlaid circle marks κ = 1/2 at the Einstein radius.
  • The singular central pixel is softened for rendering.

Headline values

  • Velocity dispersion: 300.0000 km/s; Einstein radius: 1.297795 arcsec.
  • Convergence on the Einstein circle: κ = 1/2.
  • Grid: 181 × 181 pixels over a 6.0000-arcsec field.
  • The other Beat 7 command uses 180 km/s and gives Einstein radius 0.467206 arcsec.

Notes for evaluation

The lensing models are idealized thin lenses at fixed illustration distances. The rendered intensity maps use false colour, and the ray panels are schematic; image counts within the documented near-caustic buffer are not promised. The ideal point-source image positions and signed magnifications in Beats 1–2 appear in each run’s provenance sidecar; their finite-width rendered spots only approximate those positions.
