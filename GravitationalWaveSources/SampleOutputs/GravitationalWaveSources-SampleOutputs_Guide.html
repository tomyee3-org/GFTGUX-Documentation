GravitationalWaveSources

Representative Sample Outputs

Current program version: 1.7.0   Build: 54e086464f39
Guide prepared: 2026-09-04

Purpose. These three figures provide a compact visual survey for Prof. Bernard Schutz and Vince Higgs. GravitationalWaveSources integrates the leading-order quadrupole frequency evolution of a quasi-circular compact binary from a selected starting frequency to the Schwarzschild innermost-stable-circular-orbit (ISCO) cutoff. It plots an illustrative strain scale, its amplitude envelope, and the instantaneous gravitational-wave frequency. An optional, explicitly simplified Schwarzschild quasi-normal-mode (QNM) extension illustrates ringdown after the cutoff.

1. Default binary-neutron-star inspiral

python main.py --outdir ./figures

The program’s unmodified default output, generated with version 1.7.0 and embedded directly in this guide.

What it shows

  • The complete approximately 158-second inspiral of two 1.4-solar-mass objects, starting at a gravitational-wave frequency of 20 Hz.
  • The rapidly oscillating waveform and its dashed positive and negative amplitude envelope in the top panel.
  • The leading-order inspiral amplitude scale by itself in the middle panel.
  • The accelerating frequency sweep toward the Schwarzschild ISCO cutoff in the bottom panel.

At this full-duration scale the thousands of waveform cycles cannot be distinguished individually. Their dense overlap produces the blue wedge in the top panel. That compression makes the long, relatively quiet early inspiral and the abrupt-looking late chirp visible in a single figure.

Headline values

  • Component masses: 1.400 + 1.400 M☉; chirp mass: 1.2188 M☉.
  • Distance: 400.0 Mpc; starting frequency: 20.0 Hz.
  • Time to ISCO: 157.868 s; ISCO frequency: 1570.4 Hz.
  • Face-on strain-amplitude scale at ISCO: 5.583 × 10−23.
  • Stored inspiral samples: 789,341.

2. Heavy binary-black-hole inspiral

python main.py --m1 36 --m2 29 --d 440 --outdir ./figures

A short, heavy-binary inspiral in which individual waveform cycles are visible across the full run.

What it shows

  • A 36 + 29 M☉ system, chosen as a pedagogically familiar mass combination associated with GW150914, without claiming to reproduce that observed event.
  • The waveform cycles crowd together while the amplitude rises, making the meaning of “chirp” immediately visible.
  • The larger chirp mass drives a much faster sweep than in the default neutron-star case.
  • The larger total mass produces a much lower Schwarzschild ISCO frequency.

Headline values

  • Component masses: 36.000 + 29.000 M☉; chirp mass: 28.0956 M☉.
  • Distance: 440.0 Mpc; starting frequency: 20.0 Hz.
  • Time to ISCO: 0.813 s; ISCO frequency: 67.6 Hz.
  • Face-on strain-amplitude scale at ISCO: 1.165 × 10−21.
  • Stored inspiral samples: 4,065.

3. Zoomed illustrative black-hole ringdown

python main.py --m1 36 --m2 29 --d 440 --ringdown --t_before 0.2 --t_after 0.05 --outdir ./figures

The final 0.2 s before the ISCO cutoff and the available data up to 0.05 s afterward, with the optional QNM extension enabled.

What it shows

  • The blue leading-order inspiral ends at the dotted ISCO-cutoff line; the red damped oscillation begins immediately afterward.
  • The program attaches the ringdown continuously in strain and phase, while the middle and bottom panels correctly stop at the end of the inspiral model.
  • The QNM oscillation has a higher frequency than the inspiral’s ISCO cutoff and decays over several milliseconds.
  • The caveat “illustrative ringdown — not a physical merger” appears on the plot itself so it remains visible when the image is separated from the documentation.

Headline values

  • Inspiral values are the same as in Sample 2: 0.813 s to a 67.6 Hz ISCO cutoff.
  • Illustrative final mass: 61.750 M☉, or 95% of the initial total mass.
  • Schwarzschild QNM frequency: 195.5 Hz.
  • QNM amplitude-decay time: 3.417 ms.

Notes for evaluation

  • The physical inspiral ends at the Schwarzschild ISCO cutoff. The program does not model the strongly relativistic merger.
  • The plotted amplitude is the Newtonian quadrupole face-on amplitude scale. It is not a sky-and-polarisation average or the response of a particular detector.
  • The model omits spin, eccentricity, source inclination, detector antenna response, cosmological redshift, higher harmonics, neutron-star tides, and higher-order post-Newtonian corrections.
  • The optional ringdown assumes a nonrotating remnant with 95% of the initial total mass. It is a teaching illustration, not a general inspiral-merger-ringdown waveform.
  • Every run prints the component and chirp masses, distance, frequency range, time to ISCO, strain scale, sample count, and—when enabled—QNM quantities.
  • --outdir PATH saves a timestamped PNG in addition to displaying the interactive figure. --csvdir PATH similarly exports a self-documenting numerical table.
