Beat 4 — A readable 36+29 solar-mass chirp

Exact command:

python main.py --m1 36 --m2 29 --d 440 --outdir ./figures

What it shows

  • The top panel combines signed strain with its expanding amplitude envelope.
  • The middle panel isolates the positive inspiral amplitude scale.
  • The lower panel shows increasing wave frequency up to the cutoff at the right edge; the dashed horizontal line marks f_ISCO.

Headline values

  • Masses: 36.000 + 29.000 M☉; chirp mass: 28.0956 M☉.
  • Distance: 440.0 Mpc; start: 20.0 Hz.
  • Time to ISCO: 0.813 s; cutoff: 67.6 Hz.
  • Face-on amplitude scale at cutoff: 1.165e-21; 4,065 inspiral samples.

Beat 5 — The same binary at twice the distance

Exact command:

python main.py --m1 36 --m2 29 --d 880 --outdir ./figures

What it shows

  • The printed amplitude is half that of the preceding 440-Mpc example.
  • Autoscaled vertical axes can make the two waveform plots look similar.
  • The duration and cutoff frequency are unchanged in this model, which omits cosmological redshift.

Headline values

  • Masses: 36.000 + 29.000 M☉; chirp mass: 28.0956 M☉.
  • Distance: 880.0 Mpc; start: 20.0 Hz.
  • Time to ISCO: 0.813 s; cutoff: 67.6 Hz.
  • Face-on amplitude scale at cutoff: 5.823e-22; 4,065 inspiral samples.

Beat 7 — A zoomed illustrative Schwarzschild ringdown

Exact command:

python main.py --m1 36 --m2 29 --d 440 --ringdown --t_before 0.2 --t_after 0.05 --outdir ./figures

What it shows

  • The dotted cutoff separates blue inspiral from red illustrative ringdown; they join at the last inspiral trough. The annotation box hides the top of the first ringdown peak; in the Matplotlib window, zoom to roughly 0.812–0.834 s to inspect it.
  • The middle and bottom panels stop at the cutoff and describe inspiral quantities only.
  • The annotation identifies the red extension as illustrative rather than a physical merger.

Headline values

  • Inspiral ends after 0.813 s at 67.6 Hz.
  • Assumed nonspinning final mass: 61.750 M☉.
  • Illustrative QNM frequency: 195.5 Hz.
  • QNM amplitude-decay time: 3.417 ms.

Notes for evaluation

  • Execute each exact command in the folder containing main.py. The figure is displayed and a PNG is also saved.
  • Hover over a Matplotlib graph to read cursor coordinates in the toolbar. Use a short zoom interval to measure oscillations.
  • Compare numerical amplitudes and vertical-axis scales when comparing distances.
  • The blue inspiral ends at the adopted Schwarzschild ISCO; the red extension is a prescribed illustrative QNM, not a computed merger.
  • --csvdir ./data exports numerical samples and run metadata in columns t_s,f_hz,A,h,phase_rad.
