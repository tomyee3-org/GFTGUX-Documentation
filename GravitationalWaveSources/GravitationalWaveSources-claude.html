The question for this laboratory: Why can the same two bodies set both a rising frequency and a finite cutoff, yet not tell us a physical merger waveform? Run each beat before using its worked explanation.

Before the first run

Use Python 3.10 or later with NumPy and Matplotlib. In the directory containing main.py, run python main.py --version and compare it with the version/build at the top of this page. Each beat command opens a Matplotlib figure; --outdir ./figures also saves a PNG. --csvdir ./data writes a timestamped CSV containing metadata and the columns t_s,f_hz,A,h,phase_rad. The default 1.4+1.4 solar-mass run exports roughly 789,000 rows; for small CSV experiments begin at --f_start 100 instead. Use python main.py --help to inspect all parameters.

Reading the panels: The top panel is the strain h(t); the middle panel is its inspiral amplitude scale A(t); the bottom panel is the gravitational-wave frequency f(t). A full 158-second plot cannot resolve individual late-time cycles at ordinary screen width; use Matplotlib's zoom or the --t_before window to inspect them. The lower-right Matplotlib toolbar shows cursor coordinates in the current axes.

Beat 0 · The signal grows faster and louder

Three tasks. (1) Run the equal-mass default and record the chirp mass, time to the cutoff and cutoff frequency from the terminal. (2) Locate the rising frequency in the bottom panel and the rising amplitude scale in the middle panel. (3) Identify where the upper waveform stops. Would a longer recording of this model contain a merger?

python main.py --outdir ./figures

Check your run. The 1.4+1.4 solar-mass pair at 400 Mpc begins at 20 Hz. The console prints a chirp mass of 1.2188 M☉, 157.868 s to the cutoff, f_ISCO=1570.4 Hz and a face-on strain-amplitude scale A_ISCO=5.583e-23. The waveform is stopped deliberately at the Schwarzschild ISCO. At full extent, individual cycles may be too dense to distinguish; use --t_before 0.05 to inspect the last 0.05 s on a separate run.

DERIVED The binary's chirp mass is Mc=(m1 m2)^(3/5)/(m1+m2)^(1/5). The leading-order quadrupole equation integrated here is df/dt=(96/5)π^(8/3)(G Mc/c³)^(5/3) f^(11/3), alongside dΦ/dt=2πf. Both frequency and phase advance with RK4. The printed chirp mass is the mass combination controlling that frequency sweep; it does not determine the Schwarzschild cutoff alone.

DERIVED WITHIN THE MODEL The inspiral envelope is A=4(G Mc)^(5/3)(π f)^(2/3)/(c⁴ d) for the chosen face-on quadrupole scale and luminosity distance d. This is neither a sky/polarization average nor the measured response of a particular detector. Doubling the distance halves the displayed envelope but leaves the frequency evolution unchanged.

Beat 1 · A heavier pair chirps through the band sooner

Three tasks. (1) Compare the time to cutoff with Beat 0. (2) Compare both the chirp masses and cutoff frequencies. (3) Zoom the top panel if needed and decide whether a shorter waveform alone tells you the effect of chirp mass independently of total mass.

python main.py --m1 36 --m2 29 --d 440 --outdir ./figures

Check your run. A 36+29 solar-mass pair at 440 Mpc has Mc=28.0956 M☉; it goes from 20 Hz to its cutoff in 0.813 s (4,065 inspiral samples). The cutoff is 67.6 Hz and the face-on amplitude scale there is 1.165e-21. These two runs change the chirp mass, total mass and distance, so the strain amplitudes do not isolate any one change. Use a fixed-total-mass experiment below.

DERIVED The chosen cutoff is f_ISCO=c³/[6^(3/2)πG(m1+m2)]: its mass dependence is through the total mass, unlike the frequency sweep controlled by Mc. For a finite starting frequency, T=(5/256)π^(-8/3)(G Mc/c³)^(-5/3) [f_start^(-8/3) − f_ISCO^(-8/3)] gives a leading-order analytic time check. The program's reported time ends with a linear interpolation across the final RK4 step, so very fine comparisons of cutoff times need care.

Beat 2 · The red signal is a teaching extension

Three tasks. (1) Find the dotted ISCO line where the blue inspiral ends and the red damped oscillation begins. (2) Read the frequency and decay time for that oscillation. (3) Explain why the middle and bottom panels do not continue through the red segment.

python main.py --m1 36 --m2 29 --d 440 --ringdown --t_before 0.2 --t_after 0.05 --outdir ./figures

Check your run. The inspiral still cuts off after 0.813 s at 67.6 Hz. The assumed nonrotating final mass is 61.750 M☉ (95% of the original 65 M☉). The illustrative quadrupolar QNM has f_QNM=195.5 Hz and an amplitude-decay time of 3.417 ms. Its strain and phase meet the inspiral continuously at the seam; a physical binary merger and its matching energy flux are not modeled. The red segment has no inspiral f or A values, so neither the middle nor bottom inspiral panel should extend through it.

PRESCRIPTION The assumed remnant is M_final=0.95(m1+m2) with zero spin. Within this Schwarzschild teaching model the chosen fundamental mode obeys (G M_final/c³) ω ≈ 0.3737 − 0.0890 i, hence f_QNM=0.3737/[2π(G M_final/c³)] and τ=(G M_final/c³)/0.0890. These formulae scale as f_QNM∝1/M_final and τ∝M_final. The figure itself labels the extension “illustrative ringdown — not a physical merger”; do not present it as a measured waveform.

Graded experiments

  1. EXP-1 · Observe: In Beat 0, read Mc, f_ISCO and the 157.868 s duration. Use the three panel labels to distinguish strain, its envelope and frequency.
  2. EXP-2 · Distance: Rerun with --d 200 and --d 800. With the original 400 Mpc, compare the printed ISCO strain scales; predict the 200 Mpc value 1.117e-22 (twice 5.583e-23 to displayed precision). Check whether 157.868 s and 1570.4 Hz change.
  3. EXP-3 · Band entrance: Repeat the default with --f_start 30 and 40. Compare the printed durations with the finite-frequency inspiral-time expression from Beat 1. Do not infer an exact power law while the ISCO term is finite.
  4. EXP-4 · Chirp mass versus total mass: Compare Beat 0 and Beat 1 with their printed masses and cutoff values. Identify which two mass combinations change; an attribution to chirp mass alone is not justified by these two runs.
  5. EXP-5 · Fixed total mass: Compare python main.py --m1 20 --m2 20 --f_start 40 and python main.py --m1 30 --m2 10 --f_start 40. Both have 40 M☉ total, so f_ISCO must agree; record the distinct chirp masses, durations and ISCO strain scales.
  6. EXP-6 · Numerical convergence: Use --m1 36 --m2 29 --d 440 --f_start 20 at --dt 0.0002 and --dt 0.0001 with --csvdir ./data. Compare interpolated f and phase_rad at a matched interior time such as t=0.4000 s, well before cutoff; do not use the final interpolated cutoff time alone to infer RK4 order.
  7. EXP-7 · Infer chirp mass: Run python main.py --f_start 100 --csvdir ./data. Read the first two distinct CSV times and frequencies, compute an estimate of df/dt=(f₂−f₁)/(t₂−t₁) from those rows. Take f_mid=(f₁+f₂)/2 and use physics_gw.chirp_mass_from_fdot(f_mid, fdot_est)/physics_gw.M_sun. Expect approximately 1.2188 M☉ with sufficiently close rows; the CSV includes 17-digit times and a metadata header. The finite difference is an approximation, not the exact derivative.
  8. EXP-8 · Phase: In the same CSV, find a short interval near 100 Hz. Estimate its phase increase as 2π f Δt and compare with the stored phase_rad difference. Explain why the approximation worsens as f changes faster.
  9. EXP-9 · The seam: Use Beat 2's zoom and report what each panel does at the dotted cutoff. Why is continuity of strain not a claim of a physical merger?
  10. EXP-10 · QNM scaling: With equal masses, compare python main.py --m1 20 --m2 20 --ringdown --f_start 40 and python main.py --m1 40 --m2 40 --ringdown --f_start 40. Before running, predict a factor of two decrease in f_QNM and factor of two increase in τ.
  11. EXP-11 · Source versus detector: Label each of these as included or absent: face-on quadrupole scale, spin, sky averaging, detector response, tidal effects, cosmological redshift and a fully modeled merger. Use the limitations below to check your list.

Parameters, outputs and method

The CLI accepts component masses --m1 and --m2 (solar masses), luminosity distance --d (Mpc), step --dt (seconds), starting GW frequency --f_start (Hz), and the optional --ringdown. For ringdown, --n_tau sets the number of decay times and --rd_pts the stored points: the chosen pair must provide at least eight samples per QNM cycle; --rd_pts also lies from 2 to 500,000. Zoom only the display with --t_before and --t_after, measured in seconds from cutoff; --lw changes line width and --dpi accepts integers 1 through 600. See --help for defaults and all other input bounds.

The integrator solves the leading-order f,Φ system with RK4 and linearly interpolates the final step to the ISCO cutoff. Printed times and amplitudes are rounded; --csvdir writes the underlying samples with a provenance header. The BUILD_ID is a hash of the four core Python source files after universal-newline normalization (CRLF and LF produce the same identifier); edits to the Help, guide and tests do not change it. Build identifier provenance: version and build together distinguish a named model release from its particular core-source revision.

Each requested CSV or PNG is published as a complete file under a new name in an ordinary, trusted output directory. This is not a transaction across CSV and PNG. If an untrusted co-tenant can modify the same directory, the pathname publication has a race after its identity check; use a directory you control. Hard-link support is required. Diagnostic permission-probe warnings go to standard error. An interrupted run may leave private temporary files named .gw_tmp_* or empty permission-probe files .gw_modeprobe_*, for either CSV or PNG export; once the process is no longer writing, these leftovers can be deleted.

What this model can and cannot claim

The leading-order quasi-circular quadrupole inspiral is an idealized calculation, not a precision waveform template. Spin, eccentricity, source inclination, sky/polarization averaging, antenna response, cosmological redshift, higher harmonics, neutron-star tides and higher post-Newtonian corrections are absent. The optional Schwarzschild QNM extension has a prescribed nonspinning remnant; the binary merger is not computed. Even for a valid run the interpolated ISCO end time does not inherit a clean fourth-order convergence rate from the interior RK4 steps.

The constant GM_sun_nominal=1.3271244e20 m³/s² is the nominal solar mass parameter used here; M_sun is computed as GM_sun_nominal/G, not rounded in storage. This makes hand calculations with a differently rounded solar mass differ slightly in their last displayed digits.
