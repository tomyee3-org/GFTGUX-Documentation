One idea to carry through the four runs: a drawing of space, a local tidal measurement, a falling clock and a globally defined horizon answer four different questions about a black hole. Run each command before reading its explanation; the printed summary supplies a check on your interpretation.

Before the first run

Install NumPy and Matplotlib, open a terminal in the folder containing main.py, and run python main.py --version. The current version and build must match the line above. A normal run displays a Matplotlib figure. --outdir ./figures also saves a timestamped PNG; --csvdir ./data also writes full-precision CSV. For a data-only run, use --no_plot --csvdir ./data together. The program rejects --no_plot alone because it would produce no output.

Lengths labelled r_s are Schwarzschild radii of the stated mass. In horizons, r_s0 is the radius of the initial mass, so its plotted length and advanced-time scales do not change when M1 changes. These four plots are models, not telescope images.

Beat 0 · A spatial throat is not a tunnel

Question: Where does the upper profile become vertical, and what does the dashed lower branch mean?

python main.py --mode embed --M 10 --outdir ./figures

Find the throat at the left of the radial profile and at the narrow circle of the three-dimensional surface. The program gives r_s = 29.53 km and plots from 1 to 8 r_s. The lower dashed profile is a mirror for viewing the shape; this program draws one exterior spatial slice, not a traversable wormhole. The vertical tangent is a property of the embedding, not a singularity in the intrinsic geometry.

DERIVED For the constant Schwarzschild-time, equatorial spatial slice, z(r) = 2 sqrt[r_s (r-r_s)] for the upper branch. Its Gaussian curvature is K = -r_s/(2r³), negative even though the picture looks like a bowl. The printout quotes K(r_s) = -5.7328e-10 m⁻² at the default mass. The plotted height is an embedding coordinate in an auxiliary Euclidean space, not a distance above the hole.

Check: The dashed branch is a drawing convention; proper radial distance and coordinate difference r-r_s are different quantities. The CSV option exposes both for a comparison.

Beat 1 · A larger horizon can have gentler tides

Prediction: If you compare a 10-solar-mass hole with a 4.31-million- solar-mass one at each hole's own horizon, which has the smaller local stretching acceleration for the same short test separation?

python main.py --mode tidal --M 10 --compare_masses 10,4.31e6 --outdir ./figures

The first two panels show the radial stretching and transverse compression for M=10, over 1.01–8 r_s. The comparison panels extend across the two listed masses. Read a_r(r_s) and r_crit/r_s in the terminal table: approximately 1.8914e7 g, 57.4 for 10 solar masses, and 1.0182e-4 g, 0.01006 for 4.31 million solar masses.

EXACT COEFFICIENT / LINEARIZED SEPARATION Local geodesic deviation gives a_radial = 2GM δℓ/r³ and the magnitude a_transverse = GM δℓ/r³. At r_s=2GM/c², the result scales as M⁻² for fixed proper separation δℓ. The 100 g cutoff is a chosen illustrative threshold; r_crit<r_s means that cutoff is not reached before crossing, not that a person is known to survive. A 1.8 m body is treated only to leading order in its size relative to local curvature scales.

Check: The tangential magnitude is half the radial one. The signed eigenvalues are +2, −1, −1 in the same units: one stretching direction and two compressing directions.

Beat 2 · One fall, two clocks

Question: Does the infaller's elapsed time diverge as the last sample approaches the horizon? Which displayed time does?

python main.py --mode infall --M 10 --r0_rs 6 --outdir ./figures

The first panel plots radius against the infaller's proper time τ; the second compares Schwarzschild coordinate time t with τ. The last two panels show speed relative to local static observers outside the horizon and the received/emitted frequency ratio for a radially outward signal received at infinity. At the default stopping radius 1.0005 r_s, the summary gives τ=2.2048475 ms, t=3.3868254 ms, final local speed 0.999700 c, and frequency ratio 2.7377e-4. These are values at a stopping radius, not values at the horizon. Static observers cannot stand at the horizon.

ODE A particle released from rest at r0 obeys (dr/dτ)² = c²(r_s/r − r_s/r0) and dt/dτ = E/(1−r_s/r), with E=sqrt(1−r_s/r0). The program integrates to a stopping radius strictly above r_s; t tends to infinity as that radius tends to r_s in this coordinate system, while τ stays finite. This is a coordinate effect, not a physical halt of the particle. The frequency ratio combines gravitational redshift with the Doppler effect for the receding emitter.

Check: Do not interpret a finite displayed t at 1.0005 r_s as the time of horizon crossing; the integration deliberately stops before that event.

Beat 3 · A horizon can respond before accretion

Prediction: If the hole begins growing only at advanced time v/r_s0=5, which plotted horizon can already differ from the initial radius at v/r_s0<5?

python main.py --mode horizons --M0 5 --M1 10 --outdir ./figures

The mass doubles during 5≤v/r_s0≤15. The left panel contrasts the apparent horizon, tied to the local mass function, with the event horizon, defined by the ray that separates escape from non-escape given the future history. The right panel draws nearby outgoing null rays. The early plotted event-horizon radius is r_EH(v_start)/r_s0=1.00000145 at v_start/r_s0=−20; that tiny displacement is physical at the chosen early event. A different --v_start_margin_rs0 selects a different event, rather than refining one numerical estimate.

PRESCRIBED MASS / ODE This Vaidya model prescribes a nondecreasing, piecewise-linear M(v). Its apparent horizon is r_AH(v)=2GM(v)/c². In geometrized units the outgoing null generator obeys dr/dv = (1−2M(v)/r)/2. The reported event horizon is integrated backward from the exact future condition r_EH(v2)=r_s1. It coincides with the apparent horizon once accretion has ended, and sits outside it earlier in this model. The curve is a global prediction using the chosen future mass history, not a signal travelling backward in time.

Check: The plotted event horizon comes from the backward integration. The forward shooting search is only a diagnostic; its narrow bracket alone does not certify that its whole trial curve matches the reported one.

Reading the numerical diagnostics

For a quick data-only comparison, run the same horizons command with --no_plot --csvdir ./data --bisect_iters 10, then repeat with --bisect_iters 60. Compare residual_rs0 (forward search bracket width), shooting_vs_backward_rs0 (difference at the starting radius), and shooting_vs_backward_curve_max_rs0 (maximum difference over the portion of the forward curve reached). Also check shooting_reached_v2. A tiny bracket or starting difference does not imply that a forward integrated null ray stayed close to the backward curve. --n_steps changes the output grid, not the internal adaptive integration. These comparisons do not by themselves bound the backward integrator's absolute error.

Methods and parameter map

  • --mode embed: --M, --r_max_rs, --n_r; closed-form spatial embedding and a sampled proper distance integral.
  • --mode tidal: --M, --r_min_rs, --r_max_rs, --n_r, --separation, --limit_g, optional --compare_masses; local tidal coefficients and an illustrative threshold.
  • --mode infall: --M, --r0_rs, --r_stop_rs, --n_points, --step_frac; RK4 track. Require 1<r_stop_rs<r0_rs.
  • --mode horizons: --M0, --M1, --v1_rs0, --duration_rs0, --v_start_margin_rs0, --v_end_margin_rs0, --n_steps, --bisect_iters, --n_family. Require M1≥M0; v1_rs0 may be negative. Integration uses the time offset relative to the start of accretion.

Mode-specific options not belonging to the selected mode are ignored. For exact defaults and input validation, consult python main.py --help. Each CSV includes its own version, build, mode and effective parameters.

Experiments and checks

  1. EXP-1, read the throat. Repeat Beat 0 and identify the throat on both panels. Explain why the dashed branch is a mirror for drawing rather than a route through a second exterior.
  2. EXP-2, compare tides. Repeat Beat 1. Which mass reaches the chosen 100 g threshold before crossing its horizon? Use r_crit/r_s, not the size of the hole, to answer.
  3. EXP-3, clocks. Repeat Beat 2 with --r_stop_rs 1.01 and then 1.0005. Which elapsed time increases sharply as the stop moves closer to the horizon? Use the two printed summaries, and state that the two stops are different physical endpoints.
  4. EXP-4, horizons. Run Beat 3 with --csvdir ./data. In the main horizons CSV, compare r_EH_m and r_AH_m at rows just before v1, and after v2. Expected: before accretion the event horizon is already outside the apparent horizon, though the gap can be very small; after accretion the two coincide by construction. Do not use the forward family CSV as the event horizon.
  5. EXP-5, spatial distance. Run python main.py --mode embed --M 10 --csvdir ./data. Compare proper_radial_distance_m to r_m-r_s at 2 r_s and 8 r_s (the default grid includes both). Use the first row’s r_m for the unrounded r_s. Use 100×[proper−coordinate]/coordinate for a percentage; state this denominator.
  6. EXP-8, tidal scaling. Repeat Beat 1 with --compare_masses 10,100,1000 and --csvdir ./data. The CSV comparison rows provide a_radial_horizon_g; compute the ratio for successive factors of ten in mass. Expected: a factor of 100 decrease, since a(r_s)∝M⁻². Explain why r_crit/r_s<1 is not a physiological survival guarantee.
  7. EXP-10, the horizon's memory. Run python main.py --mode horizons --M0 5 --M1 10 --v_start_margin_rs0 5 --no_plot --csvdir ./data, then repeat with margins 10, 15 and 20. In each main CSV, subtract 1 from the first r_EH_over_rs0. It should decrease as you select earlier events in the static past. It is a different physical displacement at each selected time, not the convergence of an error estimate. Prefer CSV precision over the rounded printed starting radius for this exercise.
  8. EXP-11, an independent clock. Repeat Beat 2 with --no_plot --csvdir ./data. For r0=6r_s and r_stop=1.0005r_s, put η=acos(2r_stop/r0−1) in the closed form τ=sqrt[r0³/(8GM)]×(η+sin η). Use the program's IAU nominal solar mass parameter GM☉=1.3271244e20 m³/s², so GM=10 GM☉; using the program's separate tabulated G and kilogram M_sun instead shifts this check by about 2.6e−4 relative. Expected: 2.2048475035 ms, within 1e−5 relative of the run's printed proper time.
  9. EXP-13, test the forward diagnostic. Repeat Beat 3 with --no_plot --csvdir ./data --bisect_iters 10 and --no_plot --csvdir ./data --bisect_iters 60. Record whether the forward trial reaches v2 and its maximum curve difference. Why does bracket-width improvement alone fail to measure the accuracy of the plotted event horizon?
  10. EXP-18, join the four views. Run all modes at a shared mass of 10 solar masses (set --M0 10 --M1 10 for horizons). Connect the radius and horizon Gaussian curvature in the embed summary, the horizon tidal acceleration, the proper infall time, and the static event/apparent horizon coincidence. Use K(r_s)=−1/(2r_s²) and r_s∝M to show why curvature and tidal acceleration at a fixed test separation both scale as M⁻².

Where the model stops

The embedding draws one constant-time spatial slice and says nothing about traversability. Tidal accelerations apply geodesic deviation to a small proper separation; material failure is absent. Infall is radial motion of a point test particle; its Schwarzschild time is a coordinate that becomes singular at the horizon. Vaidya accretion is prescribed ingoing null dust, not a simulated star or disk. Both the horizon's early displacement and the forward shooting instability can become too small or large to resolve with finite precision; the program reports its computational-domain failures rather than guaranteeing every extreme input will work.
