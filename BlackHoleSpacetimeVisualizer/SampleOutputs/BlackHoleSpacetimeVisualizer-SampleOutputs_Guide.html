Purpose. These four figures give a compact visual survey of all four program modes. The program explores exact Schwarzschild geometry and, for an accreting black hole, the ingoing Vaidya generalisation. Closed-form quantities are evaluated directly; the infall and event-horizon trajectories use numerical integration of the stated geodesic equations. The physical scenarios are deliberately idealised for teaching.

2. Tidal forces: stellar-mass versus supermassive holes

Figure: BlackHoleSpacetimeVisualizer-Sample2-Tidal.png

python main.py --mode tidal --M 10 --compare_masses 10,4.31e6 --outdir ./figures

Generated directly from BlackHoleSpacetimeVisualizer 1.4.0, build 100876a488a0.

What it shows

  • Radial stretching and tangential compression versus radius for a 1.8 m test separation.
  • The cross-mass panels show the inverse-square mass scaling of the horizon tide and whether the illustrative 100 g threshold lies outside or inside the horizon.
  • The comparison gives a direct quantitative demonstration that a supermassive horizon can be locally gentle.

Headline values

  • For 10 solar masses: radial horizon tide = 1.8914e7 g; the 100 g threshold is reached at 57.4 r_s, outside the horizon.
  • For 4.31e6 solar masses: radial horizon tide = 1.0182e-4 g; the threshold radius is 0.01006 r_s, inside the horizon.

3. Radial infall: two clocks and an outward signal

Figure: BlackHoleSpacetimeVisualizer-Sample3-Infall.png

python main.py --mode infall --M 10 --r0_rs 6 --outdir ./figures

Generated directly from BlackHoleSpacetimeVisualizer 1.4.0, build 100876a488a0.

What it shows

  • Four coordinated panels show radius, Schwarzschild coordinate time, local speed, and outward redshift along one radial fall.
  • The particle reaches the stopping radius in finite proper time, while the distant-observer coordinate time grows rapidly.
  • The outward signal becomes progressively redshifted as the particle approaches the horizon.

Headline values

  • Released from rest at 6 r_s and stopped at 1.0005 r_s.
  • Proper time elapsed: 2.2048475 ms.
  • Schwarzschild coordinate time elapsed: 3.3868254 ms.
  • Final local speed: 0.999700 c; final frequency ratio nu_obs/nu_emit = 2.7377e-4.

4. Apparent and event horizons in an accreting Vaidya spacetime

Figure: BlackHoleSpacetimeVisualizer-Sample4-Horizons.png

python main.py --mode horizons --M0 5 --M1 10 --outdir ./figures

Generated directly from BlackHoleSpacetimeVisualizer 1.4.0, build 100876a488a0.

What it shows

  • The first panel contrasts the local apparent horizon with the global event horizon while the mass doubles.
  • The event horizon begins to grow before the shaded accretion interval, illustrating its teleological definition.
  • The second panel shows nearby outgoing null geodesics that plunge or escape, with the event-horizon generator as their boundary.

Headline values

  • Initial and final masses: 5 and 10 solar masses.
  • Accretion window: v/r_s0 = 5 to 15; plotted range = -20 to 30.
  • At the left boundary, r_EH/r_s0 = 1.00000145.
  • The reported event horizon is obtained by backward integration from the exact post-accretion boundary condition; forward shooting is retained as a diagnostic.

Notes for evaluation

  • Each run prints a detailed terminal summary and can write full-precision, self-documenting CSV data.
  • --outdir PATH saves a timestamped PNG while retaining the on-screen display.
  • --csvdir PATH writes the numerical tables; --no_plot --csvdir PATH supports data-only parameter sweeps.
  • The accompanying help file separates exact formulae, derived quantities, numerical integrations, and modelling idealisations, and supplies exercises from introductory interpretation through numerical-method comparisons.

Image storage. The four current-build figures are embedded as indexed 256-color PNGs. Their combined payload is 337,802 bytes, reduced from 995,950 bytes in the original RGBA guide, with no material visual change at the displayed resolution.
