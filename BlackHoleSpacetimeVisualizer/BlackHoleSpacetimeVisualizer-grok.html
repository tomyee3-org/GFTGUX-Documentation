Four pictures, four questions: what space looks like on a chosen slice; how strongly nearby worldlines pull apart; whose clock measures a fall; and which outgoing light ray separates escape from capture after a black hole grows. Work through the beats in order. Let the plot and the printed summary supply the evidence before reading each equation.

Before the first run

From the folder with main.py, run python main.py --version and compare with the stamp above. Install NumPy and Matplotlib if needed. A run displays its figure; --outdir ./figures additionally saves a PNG. For a numerical table add --csvdir ./data. A CSV-only run requires both --no_plot --csvdir ./data.

Beat 0 · Draw space, not a passage

Where is the narrowest ring? What part of the picture has been mirrored?

python main.py --mode embed --M 10 --outdir ./figures

Read the radial scale first. The left panel is a cross-section; the right revolves it into Flamm's paraboloid. The dashed lower profile only helps you see the shape: the model has not opened a second exterior or a tunnel. The vertical tangent at r=r_s is part of the embedding.

The equation this picture earns is z=2 sqrt[r_s(r−r_s)] for the upper branch, with r_s=2GM/c². Its height lives in an auxiliary drawing space. If you need a distance along the surface, use the CSV's proper_radial_distance_m column instead of reading the vertical axis as a physical altitude.

Beat 1 · Compare two horizons by their tides

Before running, guess whether the larger hole has a harsher or gentler horizon for a short rod. Then read the comparison panels and summary.

python main.py --mode tidal --M 10 --compare_masses 10,4.31e6 --outdir ./figures

Radial stretching and transverse compression are magnitudes; the names carry their opposite signs. For small proper separation δℓ, the local coefficients give a_radial=2GMδℓ/r³ and a_transverse=GMδℓ/r³. At each mass's own r_s∝M, the radial magnitude scales as M⁻². The plotted 100 g cutoff is an illustrative rule, not a calculation of whether a real body survives. A ratio r_crit/r_s<1 means only that this threshold is reached after the crossing in the model.

Beat 2 · Stop the clock just short of the horizon

Which clock gives the infaller's own elapsed time, and which coordinate time grows steeply as the endpoint approaches r_s?

python main.py --mode infall --M 10 --r0_rs 6 --outdir ./figures

The run stops at 1.0005 r_s; it does not show a crossing. Read the proper time τ, Schwarzschild time t, local speed and outward light's received/emitted frequency ratio. To see why the two times separate, the radial geodesic gives (dr/dτ)²=c²(r_s/r−r_s/r0) but dt/dτ=E/(1−r_s/r), with E=sqrt(1−r_s/r0). The latter diverges at the horizon in Schwarzschild coordinates. The particle's own time stays finite. “Local static observer” has meaning only outside the horizon.

Beat 3 · Let the future choose a horizon

The hole doubles in mass. Look left of the shaded growth window: which horizon already exceeds the initial radius?

python main.py --mode horizons --M0 5 --M1 10 --outdir ./figures

The dashed apparent horizon responds to the prescribed local mass, r_AH=2GM(v)/c². The solid event horizon separates outgoing light that escapes from light that cannot, so its position uses the future mass history. Here the program integrates the outgoing null equation dr/dv=(1−2M(v)/r)/2 backward from the exact post-accretion condition r(v2)=r_s1 (geometrized units). Its early rise is a global property, not information moving backward in time. The forward shooting rays on the right are illustrations and diagnostics; they do not supply the reported event-horizon curve.

Try it yourself

  1. EXP-5: Repeat Beat 0 with --csvdir ./data. Compare the proper_radial_distance_m column with the coordinate difference r_m−r_s near 2 r_s. Which is longer, and why can the profile's bowl shape still have negative intrinsic Gaussian curvature?
  2. EXP-8: Repeat Beat 1 with --compare_masses 10,100,1000. What factor changes the horizon tide when mass grows tenfold? Locate the r_crit/r_s column before claiming anyone survives.
  3. Repeat Beat 2 with --r_stop_rs 1.01. Compare its two printed elapsed times with the default stop. They describe different endpoints; which one changes most?
  4. Repeat Beat 3 with --csvdir ./data. The main CSV has event and apparent radii: locate rows just before mass growth begins and just after it ends. Which of the two gaps is zero by construction?
  5. EXP-10: Repeat Beat 3 with --v_start_margin_rs0 5 and then 15, using --csvdir ./data. Compare the first-row r_EH_over_rs0−1 in the main CSV. Different margins select different early events on the physical horizon.
  6. EXP-11: Repeat Beat 2 and compare its printed proper time with τ=sqrt[r0³/(8GM)]×(η+sin η), where η=acos(2r_stop/r0−1) and GM uses the nominal solar mass parameter specified by the program. Does it agree to better than one part in 1e5?
  7. EXP-13: Run python main.py --mode horizons --M0 5 --M1 10 --bisect_iters 10 --no_plot --csvdir ./data, then repeat with --bisect_iters 60. The summary distinguishes the forward-search bracket width, starting-radius difference, whether the forward trial reached v2, and its largest difference from the backward curve. Which measurement actually compares curves? The small initial displacement printed at v_start is a physical value at that selected earlier event, not a truncation error.
  8. EXP-18: Repeat all four modes at 10 solar masses, setting --M0 10 --M1 10 for horizons. How do the embedding curvature and tidal acceleration at r_s change if you increase the mass while retaining the same test separation?

Boundaries of the demonstration

embed is a spatial slice, tidal uses a linearized separation without material strength, and infall is radial motion of a point test particle. The horizons mode prescribes nondecreasing ingoing null-dust accretion rather than simulating a real accretion disk. None of the modes includes rotation. Use python main.py --help for defaults, accepted parameters and additional output choices; mode-specific options from other modes do not affect the selected calculation.
