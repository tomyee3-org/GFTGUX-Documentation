Version: 1.7.0

Release Status

Version 1.7.0 follows the 1.5.0 release and its documentation-only Kickoff2 repackaging (Help rewritten as the Beats tutorial), then two further review rounds (Audit21 and Audit22, each with Codex, Grok and Copilot) and a third read-only review (Audit23) of the delivered 1.7.0 tree. Audit21 and Audit22 together raised 43 findings: 38 are repaired, 4 are repaired in part, 1 was accepted with no change needed, and none were declined.

Audit23 found no P1 defect. Codex reported two P2 and one P3 defect and judged the build “NOT READY for release as 1.7.0”; Grok found one P3 documentation gap and said it would give the build to students; Copilot found nothing. The Audit23 defects have not been fixed in this release. They are listed below as Known Bugs (KB-1 to KB-3 are Codex’s, KB-4 is Grok’s, KB-5 was found while regenerating the sample figures). All five were reproduced against the delivered 1.7.0 files. The default runs shown in the Help and the sample figures are not affected by KB-1 to KB-4; KB-5 is visible in the default polytrope figure.

Known Bugs (open)

Reproduction commands are given so that each report can be confirmed independently. Causes and fixes are deliberately not analysed here.

  • KB-1 (P2, Codex A23-P2-01): polytrope radius depends on an internal surface cutoff. For stiff-polytrope neutron stars the TOV radius changes when the internal surface cutoff y_floor is tightened from 1e-8 (the default) to 1e-10. The cutoff is not exposed through ns_mass_radius_curve() or the command line, and the program reports no warning for Γ of 1.4 or above. In the reproduction below the mass changes by 0.006%. Reproduction: 
    import physics_sev as P
eos = P.PolytropeEOS(gamma=1.40, p_nuc=1.0)
yc = eos.x_from_density(6.768e17)
P.integrate_structure(eos, yc, relativistic=True, r_scale=1.5e4, y_floor=1e-8)   # 29.1967 Msun, 1172.544 km
P.integrate_structure(eos, yc, relativistic=True, r_scale=1.5e4, y_floor=1e-10)  # 29.1984 Msun, 1257.492 km
    Radius change on tightening the cutoff, other cases checked:
    Γp_nuccentral density (kg/m³)R at 1e-8 (km)R at 1e-10 (km)change
    1.400.045e1911.14411.2430.881%
    1.401.06.768e171172.5441257.4927.245%
    1.391.05.771e171417.5571545.8919.053% (the warning quotes 0.1–0.6%)
    1.501.02.84e18285.490288.5481.071%
    The 1.40 / p_nuc 1.0 case is the maximum-mass point of a default sequence (29.1967 M☉; turning point found, causal, no warning). The warning POLYTROPE_GAMMA_SLOW_SURFACE fires only for Γ below 1.4. Help statements contradicted: Beat 9 (“0.1–0.6%”) and the Validity-table row saying polytropes are reasonably represented for Γ of about 1.4 and above. Sample-figure models (default polytrope, p_nuc 0.08, Γ = 3.0, neutron TOV) change by less than 0.0001%.
  • KB-2 (P2, Codex A23-P2-02): causality is checked only on the stable branch, which can be one model long. When the maximum mass is the first sampled model, the sequence is reported as having a stable branch that is checked for causality over a single point, and a strongly acausal sequence is not flagged. Reproduction: 
    ns_mass_radius_curve(eos_name="polytrope", gamma=2.5, p_nuc=0.04, n=20,
                     rho_lo=2e18, rho_hi=1e19, relativistic=True)
    Returns i_max 0, turning_point False, stable_branch False, causal True, cs/c 0.9274 at the maximum and on the branch. An independent scan of the same 20 models finds a maximum cs/c of 1.185920 and 16 of the 20 models acausal. The command line prints “NOT a maximum” but no ACAUSAL flag. The default --rho_lo is 1e17, so default runs are not affected. Help statements contradicted: the “whole branch” causality row in the Validity table and the matching known-artefacts bullet.
  • KB-3 (P3, Codex A23-P3-01): turnoff_mass() does not validate its inputs as a pair. Reproduction: 
    turnoff_mass([1,2,3], [10,5], 7)          # 1.4285714285714288  (lists of unequal length; no error)
turnoff_mass([-1,1,2], [20,10,5], 7)      # 1.4285714285714288  (negative mass; no error)
turnoff_mass(["1","2"], [10,5], 7)        # raw TypeError
turnoff_mass([1,2,3], [10,10,5], 10)      # 2.0  (non-strictly-decreasing lifetimes; no error)
    The only caller is build_hr_grid(), which passes clean internal arrays, so the command line is unaffected.
  • KB-4 (P3 documentation, Grok A23-P3-1): the accepted Γ range includes values where the default TOV grid already shows the mass falling. The accepted range is 4/3 < Γ ≤ 5. On the default grid (n = 8, 1e16 to 5e19 kg/m³, p_nuc 0.04): Γ 1.34 falls from 1.437 to 0.430 M☉ with no turning point; Γ 1.40 has Mmax 1.064 M☉ at 1.14e17; Γ 1.50 has 0.979 M☉ at 1.3e18. Grok asked for one sentence in Beat 9 or EXP-17. No Help text has been added.
  • KB-5 (cosmetic, found while preparing the sample figures): the mass–radius legend can cover the maximum-mass star. In the left panel of --mode nsmr the legend partly hides the star marking the maximum mass at the default polytrope, and hides it completely at Γ = 3.0. The value is still printed in the legend text and in the terminal summary.

Known Limitations

By design, or accepted and documented. These are not defects.

  • Intended for education and conceptual exploration, not research-grade modeling. Every mode is a simplified teaching model: homology relations and empirical closures for the stars; idealized zero-temperature or ideal-gas equations of state for white dwarfs and neutron stars; no rotation, magnetic fields, mass loss, convective overshoot or binary interaction. The four modes are separate models, not one continuous evolution.
  • The schematic low-mass “helium white dwarf” remnant is a single-star, mass-only heuristic. The Help has said so since 1.6.0.
  • The ideal neutron gas (--eos neutron) reaches a maximum of about 0.71 M☉ (0.7100 in 1.7.0) by design. The stiff polytrope is a toy stand-in for nuclear repulsion, not a fit to nuclear matter or observations.
  • Turning-point detection requires an interior sampled maximum and does not refine between grid points; the maximum mass is only as precise as the grid. Since 1.6.0 only TOV sequences get a turning point; Newtonian sequences report a “largest sampled mass” and are not stability-classified (the field name M_max is kept).
  • No comparison with a published white-dwarf mass–radius table. Partly addressed in 1.7.0 by independent oracle tests and a Sirius B check.
  • The Mestel cooling matching layer uses the envelope’s μe. The alternative (core value) would make the coefficient 14.2 times smaller and the default cooling age 79.8 Gyr instead of 5.6235 Gyr. The convention is stated in the Help and tested. The public option is still named --mu_e (Codex A22 asked for a rename; not done; Codex Audit23 accepted the convention).
  • White-dwarf integration is second order in step_frac, and the default step is coarse above about 1e15 kg/m³ (stated in Beat 6).
  • Chandrasekhar-limit coefficient derived in 1.7.0 (5.836 → 5.8252): MCh(μe = 2) is now 1.4563 M☉, not 1.459. The cutoff at 0.999 MCh is 1.4548 M☉, so a white-dwarf mass of 1.455 is now refused.
  • Output-collision protection (CSV, PNG, provenance sidecars) works within one process only; there is no cross-process atomic exclusive create.
  • Scientific narrative is duplicated across the terminal output, CSV/PNG provenance and the Help rather than centralized. Since 1.7.0 the synchronization tests (TestHelpClaimsAgreeAcrossLayers, 12 tests) guard it.
  • The Help loads MathJax from a public CDN; offline, the TeX is shown untypeset. The program itself needs no network.
  • Kickoff2 shipped no .py.txt transport companions, so the earlier limitation about them no longer applies.

Known Minor Maintenance Items

  • A three-way isochrone/turn-off status taxonomy (outside the mass grid; inside the grid but time-truncated; too few surviving points) is not implemented as machine-readable states; the two symptoms it would address are fixed and explained in text.
  • The boolean-type-checking sweep covers the public API (45 entry points, 408 cases, done in 1.7.0); private helpers are not swept. Numeric strings are still coerced by float() (PolytropeEOS(gamma="2.5") works); booleans are refused.
  • build_hr_grid() does not pre-validate every mass before integrating; the command line does. A direct call fails later with a clear error.
  • The HR-mode workload cap counts n_post even under --no_postms. It errs on the safe side.
  • --eos neutron silently ignores --gamma and --p_nuc, and the CSV lists them as “default”.
  • Numerical-methods refinements to the integrators (intermediate-state clamping near a white-dwarf surface; linear rather than event-detected interpolation at the TAMS crossing and the cooling-track endpoint) remain candidates for a dedicated round.
  • Plot regression tests are structurally partial; not every panel of every mode is covered.
  • HR-plot ZAMS line may clip at extreme masses, and the Sun marker is not labelled as an external reference (declined as polish).
  • Kramers-opacity validity warnings at extreme compositions (X = 0, Z = 1) were declined; off the documented path.
  • Three private-helper docstrings (_require_nonneg_x, _unique_stem, _finish) still cite Audit3 reviewer IDs. Moving them would change BUILD_ID with no behaviour change.
  • One test emits a Matplotlib UserWarning for a deliberately tiny figure; the suite promotes only RuntimeWarning to an error.
  • Beat 2 keeps six commands together by design; splitting would renumber every beat, pointer and test.
  • Outside the release: the repository copy of package/transport_codec.py has a leading-space IndentationError (Kickoff2 finding F2).
  • Retired: “duplicate isochrone ages are not rejected” (listed in 1.5.0). Duplicate ages have been rejected since 1.3.0; the Help documents it since 1.6.0.

Repaired Since 1.5.0

All of the following were reported by reviewers and are repaired in the version shown.

  • 1.6.0: Newtonian sequences never report a turning point (they report a “largest sampled mass”).
  • 1.6.0: PolytropeEOS and chandrasekhar_mass reject negative, non-finite, boolean and complex input; chandrasekhar_mass(0) no longer raises ZeroDivisionError and chandrasekhar_mass(-2) no longer returns 1.459; μe must lie in [1, 3].
  • 1.6.0: run() rejects string booleans such as postms="False".
  • 1.6.0: fifth equation tag PRESCRIPTION added; Help wording corrected (helium-white-dwarf scope among others); TestHelpQuotedNumbers ties Help numbers to real runs (repaired in part: only one Help layout ships, so the other is covered by fixtures).
  • 1.7.0: Chandrasekhar-limit coefficient derived rather than typed (5.836 → 5.8252).
  • 1.7.0: polytrope Γ restricted to (4/3, 5]; earlier any Γ > 1 was accepted, including Γ ≤ 6/5 polytropes that have no finite surface (Codex asked for 6/5; 4/3 chosen; the surface-cutoff estimate that accompanied it is KB-1).
  • 1.7.0: boolean refusal in mestel_constant, turnoff_mass and the FermiGasEOS constructor (False raised ZeroDivisionError).
  • 1.7.0: run(m_observed=...) validated in every mode (-3 was accepted and written to the provenance).
  • 1.7.0: Mestel envelope convention documented, quantified and tested (repaired in part: option not renamed); white-dwarf oracle tests added (repaired in part: no published table); cross-layer narrative tests added (repaired in part: not centralized); Help equation blocks 17 → 22.
  • Audit21 counts: Codex 17 (16 repaired, 1 in part), Grok 8 (8 repaired), Copilot 0. Audit22 counts: 18 findings (14 repaired, 3 in part, 1 accepted: a typo in a response report).
  • Earlier rounds (1.1.2 to 1.5.0) are described in the 1.5.0 notes: interior-maximum turning point, helium-ignition flag, core-mass continuity, remnant_basis, wd_structure convergence error, Fermi-gas small-x series, provenance sidecars, collision handling, CODATA 2022 constants, YEAR constant, input validation.
