Purpose. These four figures provide a compact visual survey for Prof. Bernard Schutz and Vince Higgs. StellarEvolutionTracks combines four related teaching calculations: a single stellar-evolution track, an HR-diagram grid with schematic isochrones, white-dwarf structure and cooling, and neutron-star mass–radius sequences. The modes illuminate successive topics in stellar evolution, but they are separate models rather than one continuous numerical evolution from a main-sequence star to its remnant.

1. A solar-mass evolutionary track

python main.py --mode tracks --mass 1.0 --outdir ./figures --dpi 150

A one-solar-mass teaching track from the zero-age main sequence to helium ignition, generated with version 1.5.0.

What it shows

  • The star’s path across the conventional HR diagram, whose effective-temperature axis runs from hot on the left to cool on the right.
  • Luminosity and radius histories through the main sequence, subgiant phase, and red-giant branch.
  • Central-hydrogen depletion on the main sequence and helium-core growth into the shell-burning phase.
  • The terminal-age main sequence and helium-ignition endpoint marked explicitly.

The program integrates central-hydrogen depletion and post-main-sequence core growth, but uses empirical anchors and calibrated closures for the rest of the track. It stops at helium ignition. The eventual white-dwarf entry is therefore a schematic classification from the initial mass, not a computed endpoint of the plotted evolution.

Headline values

  • ZAMS: 0.72 L☉, 0.89 R☉, and 5636 K.
  • Main-sequence lifetime: 9.73 Gyr; TAMS luminosity: 2.198 L☉.
  • Helium core at the TAMS: 0.1123 M☉.
  • Helium ignition after a further 1.988 Gyr, at a total age of 11.72 Gyr.
  • Core mass and luminosity at the endpoint: 0.4700 M☉ and 2479.2 L☉.
  • Schematic remnant classification: approximately 0.503 M☉ carbon–oxygen white dwarf, not integrated.

2. HR tracks and schematic isochrones

python main.py --mode hr --masses 0.8,1.0,1.5,2.0,3.0,5.0,10.0 --isochrones 0.1,1,5,10 --t_max 60 --outdir ./figures --dpi 150

Seven mass-dependent tracks with isochrones at 0.1, 1, 5, and 10 Gyr. The enlarged 60 Gyr main-sequence limit lets the 0.8 M☉ track reach the TAMS.

What it shows

  • The zero-age main sequence and evolutionary tracks from 0.8 to 10 M☉.
  • The strong shortening of stellar lifetimes as mass increases.
  • The turn-off point moving to lower mass and luminosity as an isochrone ages.
  • The Sun’s present position for orientation.

The isochrones are interpolants through a deliberately sparse set of simplified tracks, not replacements for a modern isochrone library. The nearly horizontal high-mass post-main-sequence branches and the change of morphology near 2 M☉ reflect explicit teaching prescriptions in the model.

Headline values

  • Seven tracks: 0.8, 1.0, 1.5, 2.0, 3.0, 5.0, and 10.0 M☉.
  • Main-sequence lifetimes range from about 19 Gyr at 0.8 M☉ to 0.03469 Gyr at 10 M☉.
  • Turn-off mass at 0.1 Gyr: 6.285 M☉.
  • Turn-off mass at 1 Gyr: 2.289 M☉.
  • Turn-off mass at 5 Gyr: 1.260 M☉.
  • Turn-off mass at 10 Gyr: 0.991 M☉.

3. White-dwarf structure and Mestel cooling

python main.py --mode wdcool --wd_mass 0.6 --outdir ./figures --dpi 150

A 0.6 M☉ carbon–oxygen white-dwarf model combining a cold Chandrasekhar structure calculation with classical Mestel cooling.

What it shows

  • The cold white-dwarf mass–radius relation, including the Earth-radius and Chandrasekhar-limit references.
  • The counter-intuitive result that more massive white dwarfs are smaller.
  • Numerical cooling luminosity approaching the asymptotic Mestel law, L ∝ t−7/5.
  • Core and surface temperatures and the cooling path far below the main sequence on the HR diagram.

Headline values

  • Requested and recovered mass: 0.6000 M☉; Chandrasekhar limit for μe = 2: 1.4590 M☉.
  • Radius: 8840.9 km, or 1.388 Earth radii.
  • Central density: 3.2070 × 109 kg m−3; mean density: 4.1217 × 108 kg m−3.
  • Surface gravity: 1.0187 × 106 m s−2.
  • Core temperature: 3.0 × 107 K to 3.0 × 106 K; effective temperature: 25,254 K to 3368 K.
  • Classical cooling age: 5.6235 Gyr; RK4 and analytic Mestel results agree to 1.56 × 10−7 relatively.

This is a strong demonstration of ideal degeneracy and the Mestel power law, but the quoted age is a model age. Real white-dwarf cooling also involves effects such as neutrino losses, crystallisation, and convective coupling.

4. Neutron-star mass–radius sequence

python main.py --mode nsmr --eos polytrope --outdir ./figures --dpi 150

A relativistic TOV sequence using the program’s default toy stiff polytrope, sampled over 40 central densities.

What it shows

  • The mass–radius relation with stable and unstable branches, a round observational benchmark near 2 M☉, and black-hole and Buchdahl reference curves.
  • The turning point of mass versus central density that identifies the maximum mass for this cold, nonrotating, one-parameter sequence.
  • Compactness and surface gravitational redshift along the sequence.
  • How an equation of state’s stiffness controls the scale of neutron-star masses and radii.

Headline values

  • Toy polytrope: Γ = 2.5 and pnuc/(ρnucc²) = 0.04.
  • Maximum mass: 2.1754 M☉.
  • Radius and central density there: 11.692 km and 1.5013 × 1018 kg m−3.
  • Compactness GM/Rc²: 0.2747; surface redshift: 0.4898.
  • Sound speed at the maximum: 0.8364 c, also the peak on the stable branch.

The TOV equations are integrated, but the chosen Γ-law is a tunable illustration rather than a realistic nuclear equation of state. The program also offers the historical ideal-neutron-gas calculation and an optional Newtonian comparison.

Notes for evaluation

  • The program distinguishes integrated differential equations, algebraically derived results, empirical fits, and calibrated closures throughout its Help file.
  • The stellar tracks stop at helium ignition, or earlier for certain low-mass cases; they do not include core-helium burning, the asymptotic giant branch, mass loss, or envelope ejection.
  • Compact-object modes are separate calculations. The program does not evolve a plotted progenitor continuously into a white dwarf or neutron star.
  • Every mode prints a detailed terminal summary and warnings when the chosen parameters expose a model limitation.
  • --outdir PATH saves a timestamped PNG and a same-stem provenance text file in addition to displaying the figure.
  • --csvdir PATH writes timestamped, self-documenting numerical tables; --no_plot --csvdir PATH supports data-only investigations.
